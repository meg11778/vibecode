What each seat is worth, by round size

Average result per hand in dollars at $1 a point, each player paid their points less the table's average. The first seat bids first and leads; the dealer bids last and may not make the bids add up to the number of tricks. The same bots sit in every seat, so any difference is the seat.

  • The dealer is the worst seat at every size.From about −$2.15 to −$2.60 at one card down to about −$0.35 at 7 or 8 cards with four players.
  • The dealer's penalty stops shrinking.Past the middle rounds it grows again: −$0.69 at 12 cards with four players.
  • The first seat gains as rounds grow.Near the bottom of the non-dealers at one card, clearly the best seat from 6 cards up.
  • These bots bid by formula.The dealer result may be the formula's weakness, not the seat's. Simulation bidding is the test still to run.

How it was measured. 120,000 deals for every square. Bids from the project's bid formula, which adjusts for the bids already made and for the hook. Cards played by the project's neural networks (the rules engine at one card, where no one has a choice). Each figure is good to about ±$0.03.

What it is not. It is the bots against themselves, not people, and not perfect play. The one exception is the one-card round, which has been solved; the solved values are shown under each map and sit within about 10 cents of the bots, 25 at the six-player dealer.

The 13-card round at four players deals the whole deck and is played at no trump, which is why it breaks the pattern of the rounds before it.
